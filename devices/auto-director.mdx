**AutoDirector** is an automated camera director. It listens to your studio microphones to follow who is speaking and **recommends** which camera should be on air and how the microphones should be mixed. AutoDirector never drives your equipment itself — it raises triggers, and your [rules](/automation/rules) turn each recommendation into a switcher or mixer command. Because the switch happens in a rule, the same director works with whatever switcher and mixer you already control in Fabl.

AutoDirector works together with [Studio Source](/devices/studio-source) devices, which represent the cameras and microphone feeds it watches.

## Connection

AutoDirector runs on a [Studio Agent](/automation/agents) and needs no network address. Configure it with:

- A **studio group** name that links it to the Studio Source devices it should watch
- A **style** that sets its pace for the kind of show — for example a two-person interview, a panel with overlapping voices, a single-speaker lecture, or a music performance, with a manual option for fine tuning
- Whether it directs the **video**, the **audio**, or **both**

Further settings tune how quickly it cuts, how long a microphone stays open after someone stops speaking, and how far unused microphones are turned down.

## Commands

Your rules can:

- **Enable** the director or hand control back to an operator
- Change its **style** or what it directs mid-show
- **Force** a specific camera and hold it, **hold** the current shot, or **release** back to automatic
- Tell the director which camera is actually on air, so it stands down when an operator takes a shot by hand
- Take a camera **in or out** of the rotation

## Triggers

AutoDirector raises triggers your rules react to, including:

- A **camera shot** being recommended, with the source to take and the suggested transition
- A **microphone move** being recommended
- The **active speaker** changing, or **overlap** and **silence** beginning or ending
- A camera **entering or leaving** the rotation
- The director **starting, stopping, or being overridden**

Trigger data such as the recommended source, its switcher input, and the microphone channel is available for use in rule actions.
